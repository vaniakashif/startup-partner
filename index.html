Why StartupPartner exists

Every week in Karachi, dozens of people pitch startup ideas. Most never build them — not because the ideas are bad, but because they can't find the right people to build with.

The problem we're solving

Finding a co-founder in Pakistan is almost entirely luck-based. You rely on your university batch, your LinkedIn network, or a chance meeting. If you're not well-connected, your idea dies in a notebook. Meanwhile, developers, marketers, and investors are looking for exciting projects to join — they just don't know where to look.

Our mission

StartupPartner democratises startup building. We believe the best ideas don't always come from well-connected people. Anyone in Karachi — a student in Orangi Town, a developer in Gulshan, an investor in DHA — should be able to find each other and build something real.

Why Karachi first

Karachi has over 20 million people, 40+ universities, and a growing tech ecosystem — but almost no infrastructure for early-stage team building. We're starting here because we know this city, and because if it works here, it works anywhere in Pakistan.

How StartupPartner works

From idea to partnership in five simple steps

1

Founder posts a startup idea

Describe your problem, solution, current stage, and what kind of partner you need. Be clear about equity and contribution expected.

2

Community members browse ideas

Filter by category, city, role, or stage. Find an idea that excites you and matches your skills or resources.

3

Submit a proposal

Tell the founder what you bring — skills, capital, or network. Write a short pitch explaining why you're the right fit.

4

Founder reviews and selects

The founder reviews all proposals and reaches out to the most suitable candidates to begin conversations.

5

Discuss, agree, and build

Use built-in chat and partnership document templates to finalise equity splits, roles, and start building together.

AgriConnect
A digital market that cuts out middlemen between Pakistani farmers and bulk buyers
AgriTechB2BLogistics

The Problem

Pakistani farmers earn only 30–40% of what their produce sells for because of a long chain of middlemen (arhtis). They have no price transparency and no direct access to mills, restaurants, or exporters. They sell low and buyers buy high — the middlemen capture all the value.

The Solution

AgriConnect is a WhatsApp-integrated platform where farmers list their harvest and buyers — mills, restaurants, exporters — bid directly. We handle logistics through partner trucks. We've piloted with 12 wheat farmers in Faisalabad with 28% better prices for them.

PayEase
A digital wallet letting unbanked workers receive, hold, and send salaries without a bank account
FintechInclusionB2C

The Problem

100 million Pakistanis have no bank account. Domestic workers, factory hands, and daily wage earners receive cash salaries that are unsafe, untrackable, and impossible to save or send remotely. Without a financial identity they can't access loans, insurance, or formal employment.

The Solution

PayEase gives every worker a mobile wallet using only their CNIC and phone number — no bank required. Employers pay salaries directly into the wallet. Workers can send money home, pay bills, and build a transaction history that unlocks microfinance products later.
